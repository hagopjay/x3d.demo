One shove. 203 courses. Every hit, heard.

The explorer from Giza Crawl stands at the edge of the Great Pyramid's summit platform, 138.5 m up, and a colleague shoves. From that instant Blender's rigid-body solver (Bullet) drives an 11-body, 80 kg ragdoll down the stepped north face. The motion is baked onto the H-Anim joints, a tracking camera follows it, and each contact that changed a body's velocity is logged with its time and impulse. This page plays each one as it happens: a thud for the trunk, a smack for a limb, a knock for the head.

How it was made

StageWhat happens
Pyramid203 stepped courses as one mesh, base 230.33 m, slope 51.84°, summit 138.5 m (platform 12.7 m square). Course heights shrink from about 1.5 m to about 0.55 m; the individual heights are generated to that published trend.
Ragdoll11 rigid bodies (pelvis, torso, head, thighs, shins, upper arms, forearms), 80 kg, joined by 10 generic constraints with anatomical angle limits; friction 0.45, restitution 0.15, 20 substeps a frame at 24 fps
Shovebodies ride a keyed tip and push for 0.5 s as kinematic bodies, then the solver takes over at 1.29 s; the pusher is a second H-Anim figure with keyed arms
Bakeeach body's motion becomes its bone's pose, frame by frame; an Empty carries the travel, the root joint keeps the local offset
Impactsfor each body and frame, impulse = mass × |Δv − g·Δt|; above 18 N·s it is a hit, at most one sound per 40 ms
Filefall.x3d, 1.0 MB, 2 HAnimHumanoids, 32 interpolators on one TimeSensor; x3d_mcp 39c6e94: XSD valid, 0 errors, 0 warnings (receipt)
GradeClaim
DocumentedBase, slope, summit height, course count and course-height trend; the 1874 summit mast (drawn schematic); Khafre and Menkaure positions.
AuthoredThe figures, the shove, the friction and restitution values, the individual course heights. Real limestone is rougher and broken: a real body would most likely stop on a ledge long before the base.
SimulatedThe path, the 197 hits, the 22.1 m/s peak speed and where the body comes to rest (12.3 m out from the base) come from Bullet with the values above, not from any measurement. The run is deterministic: the same script gives the same fall.
SoundSynthesised in the browser from each hit's kind and impulse. No samples, nothing recorded.
