three.js bridge · TSL

One scene, nine looks

The X3D file is read once. Then the same scene graph is drawn nine times a frame, each tile with a different three.js Shading Language material built from the X3D material's own colours. Nothing in the X3D changes; the look belongs to the renderer. Drag any tile to turn them all.

renderer - fps - -

Click "code" on a tile to read its TSL; double-click a tile to fill the board with it. The scenes are the Blender exports of the bridge and blenderizer showcase, read from there. The picture on each tile is drawn by your browser, live.



  

    
What stays the same

      
The X3D. The scene is a plain X3D file. The bridge's reader builds the graph (shapes, lights, camera, ROUTEs); the tiles only change which material each mesh is drawn with.

      
The colours. Every style reads the X3D material's diffuse colour, its texture or its baked vertex colours, so the same object stays recognisable in all nine.

    

    
Why TSL

      
A TSL material is a small graph of nodes. three.js compiles that one graph to WGSL on WebGPU, or to GLSL on its WebGL 2 backend when WebGPU is not there. The header pill says which one your browser got.

      
The bridge's own shader patches (a texture projector, fog coordinates, the ADD stage) are written as GLSL strings. Writing them as TSL would make them run on both. That port is not done; this page shows the toolchain working.

    

    
Honest limits

      
Shapes that the bridge draws with its own GLSL shaders (ComposedShader, volumes, 3D textures) are hidden on the tiles, because WebGPURenderer does not run raw GLSL. The bridge's headlight and X3D lights are used as they are; the styles that ignore lights say so.

      
No timing is claimed: frame rates depend on your machine.

    

  

  
Libraries: three r187 (WebGPURenderer, TSL), pinned, from jsDelivr. The X3D scenes and the bridge bundle are the ones of the neighbouring demos.
