OBrian the tropical Pacific, as plain ISO X3D · real NOAA data

OBrian is our codename for ocean and weather visualisation. This first study draws the El Niño of 2026 as it is in NOAA's own files, next to the great events of 1997–98 and 2015–16 and the La Niña of 2010–11: a 3D volume of ocean-temperature anomaly down to 300 m, the 20 °C thermocline surface, the equatorial section, the sea-surface map, wind anomalies and the Niño 3.4 box. It is one X3D file per event, animated by TimeSensor → IntegerSequencer → Switch ROUTEs, drawn by X_ITE. Every number below recomputes from the scene on screen.

Why “OBrian”. In memory of James J. O'Brien (1935–2016), Florida State University oceanographer and meteorologist, founder of the Center for Ocean-Atmospheric Prediction Studies (COAPS) and known to colleagues as “Dr. El Niño”. This study carries his codename; it is not affiliated with or endorsed by FSU, COAPS or his family. Name and dates as published by COAPS.

Honest limits

These are monthly means of an assimilation product (GODAS) and a reconstruction (ERSST v5), not single observations; the newest months (2026) are near-real-time and may be revised. Anomalies use a 2001–2020 baseline for all three feeds, so they differ from CPC's ONI, which uses a moving 30-year base (page cross-check: r and RMS above). GODAS is decimated to 2° × 1° × 10 m and the wind file ends in February 2026, so wind arrows exist only for months up to then. The vertical is exaggerated ×5,550 (300 m drawn 3 units tall, 5° of longitude per unit). X_ITE 16.7.1 was exercised only in headless Chromium with a software GL driver (SwiftShader); no frame rate or GPU result is claimed. Nothing here is a forecast.
