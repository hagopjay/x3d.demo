The bridge and the blenderizer

One standard, three ways to see it. The bridge reads X3D 4.0 nodes into Babylon.js, node by node, with a fixture, a test and a picture against X_ITE for each. The Blender add-on writes and reads X3D 4.0, and every export has to clear a validator and a render diff. Below: live scenes in two engines, the full 260-node atlas, 53 side-by-side receipts, and the gate and scorecard numbers. Each figure carries the tool that measured it.

What is not done

The point of keeping receipts is that the empty places are visible.

Bridge: 35 to do, 1 blocked

DIS (6), Geospatial (11), VolumeRendering (13) were all todo at ed1bed8; RigidBodyPhysics has 11 gaps, 2 todo and CollisionSensor blocked. Volume and physics lanes are in progress on other branches and are not counted until they merge.

Bridge: no GPU timing

Every bridge picture was drawn on a CPU rasteriser (SwiftShader). No real-GPU frame time exists and none is claimed. Many particle and emitter shots are logged gaps (ExplosionEmitter overlap 0.07, SurfaceEmitter 0.13).

Blenderizer: known limits

Text nodes are a known limit (audit D11). The gate runs on Blender 5.3.0; CI uses 5.2.2. The scorecard covers 15 scenes. Splat colours in glb (finding 4) are still open.
