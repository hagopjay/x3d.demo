About this film

    

      
The director's camera lives in the standard. Any engine can perform the film.

      
One X3D 4.0 file, odyssey.x3d, carries the whole film: the set, 35 PBR materials, 60 lights and the camera. The camera is a Viewpoint driven by three interpolators (position, orientation, field of view) at 30 keys per second, and every animated thing is clocked by one TimeSensor named Film through ROUTEs. There is no Script node. X_ITE plays the file natively; the WebGPU engine reads the same file with its own X3D loader and adds what X3D 4.0 cannot say: reflections, bloom, depth of field, volumetric light, film grain and dust.


      
The Babylon engine

      
The third engine reads the same odyssey.x3d with our own X3D reader and draws it with Babylon.js 9.32.0 on WebGPU. It is not a copy of the other two: it keeps the file's rules and lets Babylon do the rest.

      

        
Light scope
59 of the 60 lights are global="false", so each lights only the group it sits in (X3D 4.0, 17.2.1). Each is given the meshes under its parent (includedOnlyMeshes): a shape answers to the 3 to 5 lights of its own set instead of all 60, and the material light cap is never reached.

        
Light units
intensity in candela, radius as a hard range, the glTF punctual-light falloff, spot cone from cutOffAngle and beamWidth.

        
Materials
35 PBR materials with base colour, metallic-roughness (glTF packing) and normal maps; data maps are read as linear.

        
Look
The Look switch adds what the file does not say: reflections from a generated gold hall, bloom, grain, vignette, dust, and shadows from three key lights. Off, it draws the file as authored.

        
Not exact
Matching X_ITE pixel for pixel was not the aim; the gaps are listed in the run's GAPS.md.

Materials from nothing

      
Eleven materials come from the source gift; twenty-four new ones were generated by an image model (43 images, about $1.68), then made tileable with derived normal and metal/roughness maps. Every prompt is in materials/materials.json.
