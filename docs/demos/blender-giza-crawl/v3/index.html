Into the pyramid. Down the shaft. Into the legend.

The v1 route, unchanged where it is documented, rebuilt in Blender: procedural limestone, granite, bedrock, paint and gilt, lit with Cycles and baked into … image textures, under the night sky of 2560 BC drawn from the Sky Machine's own star catalogue. Each place has its own look: moonlight on the plateau, visitor lamps raking up the Grand Gallery's corbels, a cold beam in the King's Chamber, a slow descent into the dark down the Osiris Shaft, and a torch-lit Hall of Records that nobody has ever found. Every caption carries its grade: the Hall is invented, and says so.

What Blender did

Nothing here is lit by the browser except the explorer. Every surface is a texture that already holds its light: Cycles rendered each surface's albedo at full size and its light (direct and indirect, with soft shadows and colour bleeding) at a quarter of that, the two were multiplied and graded once per zone, and the result is drawn unlit. Figures are read from bake.json and grades-applied.json.

The sky

The Background is the sky over Giza in 2560 BC with Alnitak on the meridian, made from the Sky Machine's own files (Demo 65 v4: 52,602 HYG v4.1 stars and its ERFA long-term precession table) by build/sky_cube.py. It puts Alnitak …° above the southern horizon; the King's Chamber's southern shaft rises at about 45°. The cold beam in that chamber is a stylised nod to that reading: an Authored light down a documented shaft, not a claim. Receipt: sky-receipt.json.

The three cuts are honest, as in v1: no passage is known from the King's Chamber route to the Osiris Shaft, or from there to the Sphinx. The explorer is moved, the screen says so, and the clock keeps running. The moon is a stage light from azimuth 300°, as in the Sky Machine, not an ephemeris position.
