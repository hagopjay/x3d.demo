The atlas

Every claim, told in full, then tested

Two grades on every card. The number: does the arithmetic hold against the survey? The design: did the builders mean it? A claim can be exactly true and still be an accident; that is where most of the magic lives.

The shaft clock

Four shafts, four stars, four different years

Each curve is the height at which a star crossed the meridian over Giza, year by year, as the Earth's axis wobbles. Each level line is a shaft's angle. Where a curve meets its line, the shaft pointed at its star.

Solid lines: Gantenbrink's 1993 angles as commonly cited. Dashed: Petrie's 1883 angles. Geometric altitudes, no refraction. Drag the year: the stage follows.

How it was made

One chain, every link named

  1. SurveyPetrie 1883, read section by section from the Birdsall transcription; Dash 2015 for the base.
  2. Verifyverify_sacred.py and build_atlas.py compute every number on the cards. ERFA long-term precession, HYG space motion.
  3. Model + lightBlender 5.3 builds the plateau and bakes Cycles light twice: 256 samples per vertex, 96 per lightmap texel.
  4. Exportx3d-blender-io writes X3D 4.0; build_stage.py welds it, adds the proof rigs and the two layers.
  5. Gatex3d_mcp XSD and semantic checks, Loom witnesses, then X_ITE 16.7.1 with the console open.
  6. LookFrames at every stop, desktop and phone, before anything ships.

What is illustrative, not surveyed

  • Course heights and counts, and how much of each top is missing.
  • The queens' pyramids, the ground relief and the base levels (Khafre and Menkaure stand 10 m higher here).
  • The passage run and the chambers' east offset. The angles on the labels are Petrie's.
  • The Sun and Moon positions are stage lights, not an ephemeris. The stars are real.

Grades

  • Confirmed inside the survey's own error.
  • Likely close, or the source was not re-read here.
  • Suspected depends on a free choice.
  • False the survey rules it out.
  • Untested not measured in this session.
