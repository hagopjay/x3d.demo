Field notes · X3D Semantic Twin Lab · 16 Nov 2026

An ontology answers
“what is this thing?”

Daniel Beach’s essay walks the word from a 1600s monastery to today’s agent platforms. These notes keep his best ideas, test them against the nine cases we built, and add two questions a 3D twin has to answer as well.

How much?Semantic layer. Metrics, sums, joins.
What is it?Ontology. Concepts, relationships, rules.
Where is it?X3D scene. Position, size, what touches what.
Is it allowed?SHACL. Checks that name what broke.

Source: Daniel Beach, “What the !@#$% is Ontology? The new Data Mesh?”, Data Engineering Central, 16 Nov 2026. The first two rungs are his framing; the second two are ours. Grades: Confirmed measured here, Likely well-sourced but not checked here, Suspected the article’s claim or our guess, Added context the article does not give.

1 · The definition, taken apart

Three parts, and the third is optional

Beach settles on a working definition: a machine-readable model of the concepts in a domain, their relationships, and, when needed, the rules governing those concepts. Here is each part in our greenhouse case, plus the part a 3D twin adds.

Concept

A thing with a stable name that someone else publishes.

agrovoc:c_7805 skos:prefLabel "tomatoes"

From FAO AGROVOC, fetched live.

Relationship

How concepts connect, so a question can climb.

c_7805 → c_3129 → c_8174 vegetables c_9093 basil → c_3568 culinary herbs

That climb is why basil is not a vegetable.

Rule (when needed)

What must hold, written so a machine can test it.

bed is thirsty when soil water < crop need litres = gap × area × root depth

Our N3 rule and SHACL check.

Place (our addition)

Where the thing sits, so “in” and “near” can be computed.

Bed A2 twin:depictedBy sc:CropA2 CropA2 translation -3 0 2

The X3D scene, converted to RDF by x3d_mcp.

2 · The question ladder

Each layer answers one kind of question

Beach draws the line between a semantic layer (“how much or how many”) and an ontology (“what is this thing?”), adding “at least for now”. A twin needs two more rungs. Every example below is a real question from our cases.

RungHow much?
Answered bySemantic or metrics layer: a reviewed formula, reused so the number is always computed the same way.
Example“How many litres does each thirsty bed need?” (farm A1)
Without itEach agent invents its own formula and two dashboards disagree.
RungWhat is it?
Answered byOntology: published vocabularies such as AGROVOC, Brick, GS1, CIDOC-CRM, FHIR.
Example“Which beds grow vegetables?” Basil climbs to culinary herbs, not vegetables. (farm rule)
Without it“Vegetable” is a string match, and basil is miscounted.
RungWhere is it?
Answered byX3D scene: every object DEF’d, its position and size readable as triples.
Example“Whose noon shadow falls on the park at the winter solstice?” (city C1)
Without itThe records say Tower 5 exists. They cannot say what its shadow touches.
RungIs it allowed?
Answered bySHACL shapes over scene and records together; each failure names the thing and the rule.
Example“Does every room over 49 people have two ways out?” (fire code E3, IBC table 1006.2.1)
Without itA narrow door is a number nobody compared to the code.
3 · “Is it worth it?”

Beach asks for evals. Here is the first one.

His central doubt: is an agent with an ontology better than a good agent with a semantic layer? He says he would need “evals and tests” to believe it. We can start, because every case already has gold answers from the Python oracle. I reran every question, rule and check three ways: the scene alone, the records alone, and both together. Confirmed

0 / 36

Scene alone answers none of the questions or rules. Geometry without names is a pile of boxes.

27 / 36

Records alone answer three quarters. The nine they miss all ask about position, area, distance or shadow.

5 / 18

Records alone catch 5 of 18 planted faults. With the scene, all 18 are caught.

B1 answered by records aloneC1 needs the scene toorec fault caught by records alonegeo fault only visible with the scene
CaseQuestions and rulePlanted faults

Point at a chip to see the question or fault.

The fire code case shows the subtle one. “Exit stair B is locked” is a plain record fault, yet the records alone flag nothing, because the rooms that fail are the ones whose remaining exit is too far away, and distance lives in the scene. The ontology tells you the stair is locked. Only the twin tells you who is now trapped.

Caveat: we wrote these cases and planted the faults, so the set favours what this stack is built to see. It measures lift between layers on our data; it is not a general result. Scripts and results: project files, ontology-lab/eval/.

4 · Timeline

An old idea that was waiting for a user

Beach’s point is that ontology is not new; it lacked a use case until agents arrived. His dates are below in order, with a few added for context (dashed). The filled dots are our own.

c.1606

Latin ontologia appears: the study of being. Beach gives “circa 1600”. Early printed uses are usually credited to Jacob Lorhard (1606) and Rudolf Göckel (1613). Likely

1967

Computer science borrows the word for how data relates to the world. Beach cites “Another Look at Data” (1967).

1980s

Knowledge-based tools and expert systems. Stanford’s Protégé project (NIH-funded) grows from this line of work.

1993

Gruber: an ontology is “an explicit specification of a conceptualization”. The definition most papers still quote. Added

1997

DARPA push, “Toward Distributed Use of Large-Scale Ontologies”. The same year VRML97 becomes an ISO standard, the parent of X3D.

2004

OWL and RDF become W3C Recommendations; X3D becomes ISO/IEC 19775. Both of our layers are standards from the same year. Added

2017

SHACL becomes a W3C Recommendation. The “is it allowed?” rung gets a standard. Added

2024–25

SaaS vendors re-label context and semantic layers as ontology. Databricks, Snowflake, Microsoft Fabric, AWS, Palantir.

2026

Databricks Genie Ontology reaches general availability; NVIDIA and AWS publish open-source ontology builders. Per the article. Suspected

Nov 2026

Our lab: nine twins, 116 vocabulary terms each declared in the publisher’s own file, every answer checked by Python, by the MCP server and in the browser. Confirmed

7 · OntoRank, mapped to our grades

Five ways to decide which knowledge to trust

Genie Ontology ranks knowledge by five signals. This is the most transferable idea in the article, because an agent with two conflicting facts needs a reason to prefer one. Here is how each signal maps onto what the lab already does, and how strongly (the bar).

Authority

Have. Every class and property is checked against the publisher’s own vocabulary file (116 of 116).

Relevance

Have. A SPARQL question selects only what it needs; the scene highlights only those objects.

Certification

Partly. Our Confirmed / Likely / Suspected grades, and three engines that must agree. The heritage case already records disputed sources (CIDOC E13).

Popularity

Missing. Nobody uses these twins yet. Later: count which facts agents actually query.

Recency

Partly. Vocabularies carry their read date; sensor readings carry time. Records have no “last checked” yet.

Proposal: give every domain record a twin:grade and a prov:generatedAtTime, and let a SHACL check warn when an answer rests on a Suspected fact. That turns our grading habit into data an agent can read.

8 · What to take from it

Eight lessons, each turned into a move for x3d

Data has no meaning without context

Beach: the definition of a “customer” changes from one department to the next.

For us: a “room” is a BOT space, a Brick HVAC zone and an IBC occupant-load area at once. The fire case gives Room 407 an occupant load of 54 by its use, not by its walls.

Keep the format apart from the engine

Beach: a semantic-layer spec is a file format; auto-ontology is an engine that reads and writes it.

For us: X3D and Turtle are the formats, x3d_mcp is one engine. Formats outlive engines, so the bet is on the files.

Every answer comes with its working

Auto-ontology returns the SQL and the steps behind each answer.

For us: this is the tether. Each case page shows the SPARQL, the rows and the failing shape. Keep that non-negotiable.

The machine drafts, a person certifies

The common pattern: crawl and propose, humans review and approve, then serve to agents.

For us: this is the flywheel loop. Next step: an LLM drafts a domain graph from a scene, and the oracle and shapes decide what survives.

Weight kills adoption

Beach: “yet another service someone has to take care of”; COA costs money before the first question.

For us: files plus one stdio process, no server, no bill. That is a feature worth saying out loud.

The gold is in what isn’t a table

Beach’s main complaint about auto-ontology: no PDFs or documents, where most business knowledge lives.

For us: 3D scenes are a multimodal source too. Measured above, 13 of 18 faults are invisible without them. That is our niche.

Evals or it didn’t happen

Beach will not accept ontology’s value without evals that show it beats a good agent with a semantic layer.

For us: the eval set is built (45 items with gold answers). Running models on it is the next step.

Open source is thin, and the war is coming

Few serious repos; he expects an “ontology war” like the recent catalog war (Unity, Polaris, Nessie).

For us: stand on W3C and ISO standards, not a vendor’s layer. An open X3D twin lab fills a gap that is visibly empty.
