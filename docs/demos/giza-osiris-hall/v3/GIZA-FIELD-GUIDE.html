01 / First exploration

A useful scene makes its assumptions accessible.

The original study combines a schematic plateau, published measurements, reported imaging results, contested proposals, and an explicitly imagined interior. This edition adds survey and section instruments, a depth comparison, more precise sky controls, and a persistent indication of the current view's evidence and geometry status. The purpose is to make visual exploration and careful interpretation support each other.

Windows

Start the local server

Run run.bat from the extracted folder, or use py -3 serve.py. Open http://127.0.0.1:8800/, or the address printed if you choose another port. Python is required; the browser engines are bundled.

Use the same files

Run bash run.sh or python3 serve.py. Keep the server terminal open while exploring. The page needs HTTP fetches and should be served from its folder.

The optional build and test commands have their own requirements. Ordinary exploration uses the supplied scene, star catalogue and baked colours; it does not require Blender or a live generation service.

The central advance: a measured quantity, a plausible reconstruction, a graphical convention, and an imagined object can appear in the same view without becoming the same kind of claim. The interface keeps those identities available during exploration.

03 / A common spatial frame

Metres from the Sphinx, with north represented by negative Z.

The scene uses a right-handed frame: X increases east, Y increases upward, and Z increases south. The Sphinx is the local origin and the model's plateau plane is Y = 0. This is a local teaching frame; its flat datum is not a surveyed geodetic or geological reference surface.

scene point = (east, up, −north)
depth below the model datum = −Y, when Y < 0

The survey overlay adds a compass, metric rings and a visible datum. These are calibration aids for this model. A radius or a cursor readout can be exact within the constructed coordinate system even when the location of an underground feature is schematic.

Distance depends on the question

A straight-line three-dimensional distance to the Osiris mouth differs from a horizontal plan distance, a shaft-drop sum, and the distance a person could walk. Read the metric named by the interface. The model does not establish a traversable tunnel merely because two visible objects can be joined by a line.

04 / Compare quantities before comparing stories

The shaft sum and the section datum are different measurements.

Hawass's published shaft drops are 9.62 m, 13.25 m and 7.50 m. Their arithmetic sum is 30.37 m. The published section also carries a deepest label of 35.45 m relative to its stated section datum. The two numbers do not begin and end with the same measurement convention; they should not be presented as rival answers to one identically defined depth question.

9.62 + 13.25 + 7.50 = 30.37 m
section label relative to the published datum = 35.45 m

The depth theatre places these lengths on a common graphical scale so the difference is visible. That normalized comparison is not a survey cross-section connecting their endpoints. The ratio 648 / 30.37 is about 21.3; it communicates relative claimed scale and does not strengthen the underlying claim.

Keep imaging findings separate. The Great Pyramid Big Void and North Face Corridor are distinct reported features. Neither establishes a Hall of Records under the Sphinx or the proposed deep complex under Khafre.
05 / Section as an inspection instrument

Cut the display without implying an excavation.

The section control clips the rendered surface and structural groups while leaving the sky and inspection annotations available. Its plane is a mathematical display operation. It does not reveal a hidden geological volume or add survey data.

plane = (0, 0, −1, zcut)
retained half-space: Z ≤ zcut

In this frame the retained half-space is north of the cut. Move the plane slowly through the Osiris area and compare what disappears with the selected layers. A transparent ground and a clipping plane expose different aspects of the same scene: transparency keeps overlapping surfaces visible, while a section removes a half-space from the display.

Why annotations remain outside the cut

A compass, scale ruler or explanatory label is part of the instrument. Clipping those annotations with the subject would remove the reference needed to interpret the section. The generated scene therefore scopes its section plane to the model groups, while the survey and depth-theatre controls remain separate.

Try a prediction. Before moving the plane, decide which side of the model should remain. Then use the compass and the sign of Z to check your prediction.
06 / The sky model

Separate epoch, local sidereal time, and the lighting preset.

The supplied catalogue contains 978 field-star records and 40 named bright-star records. The original notice's “41 named” count was a bookkeeping error. The display combines the catalogue vectors with sampled long-term precession matrices, then converts the resulting direction into local horizon coordinates at the fixed Giza latitude.

catalogue direction → epoch rotation → local horizon direction
horizontal direction → (east, up, south) in the X3D frame

Epoch uses astronomical year numbering

Astronomical yearDisplayed historical yearReason
11 CEPositive years use their ordinary CE number.
01 BCEAstronomical numbering includes year zero.
−12 BCENegative astronomical year −n corresponds to n + 1 BCE.
−1049910500 BCEThe intended ancient preset, without the original one-year label mismatch.

The enhanced runtime interpolates orientations between the 152 supplied matrix samples using normalized rotations and spherical interpolation. It avoids the original nearest-sample century jumps and keeps the interpolated matrix orthonormal. This is a smoother representation of the supplied model; interpolation does not make the input catalogue or its neglected effects more accurate.

Sidereal time is an angle expressed in hours

Local sidereal time determines which right ascension lies on the local meridian. It is not a civil-clock time or a calendar date. The idealized equinox preset at 18 sidereal hours has a useful geometric interpretation when the Sun's right ascension is taken as zero and the horizon is idealized. It is not a date-derived sunrise calculation.

The Orion transit control chooses a sidereal orientation from the star's right ascension. A rising calculation also depends on declination and observer latitude; a star may remain above the horizon, remain below it, or graze it. The model reports those cases instead of manufacturing a rising time by forcing an impossible value into an inverse cosine.

A sky should follow the observer

The scene's observer-centred SkyRig follows camera translation while retaining celestial direction. This preserved design prevents a walk across the plateau from creating artificial parallax in a sky drawn on a finite-radius shell. The conceptual Orion-to-pyramid overlay is a separate world-space diagram, not a set of actual stars suspended above individual monuments.

Model limits remain material over millennia. This is a precession-only visualization. Proper motion, a full apparent-place pipeline, atmospheric refraction and a civil-time solar ephemeris are not supplied by the epoch slider. The effect of omitted proper motion is star-dependent. Read SKY-MODEL.md for the mathematical contract and source conventions.
07 / Baked appearance and visual judgement

The light states are authored views of the model.

Three supplied Blender Cycles runs bake illumination into vertex colours. The runtime selects their colour arrays without requiring Blender. The state called “sunrise” uses a solar elevation of +7°, so the enhanced interface describes it as an illustrative morning state. The sky orientation and light-state controls remain independent.

The baked meshes use unlit material presentation so the stored colours can carry the appearance. Exposure changes displayed brightness; it does not rerun light transport. The states have different recorded normalization rules and should not be treated as calibrated measurements of relative solar energy.

Rendering quality changes image sampling cost. It does not increase the archaeological accuracy of a chamber, add missing survey geometry, or improve the precession model. Save the camera, viewport and settings when comparing views. Read any live engine cadence as a local observation; it is not a cross-device GPU benchmark.

08 / Reproducible exploration

A viewpoint is more useful when its context travels with it.

The twelve-slot, browser-local camera notebook and session controls preserve a useful experimental configuration: view or camera, layer choices, sky epoch and sidereal time, appearance settings, and inspection controls. A camera alone does not explain a screenshot in which a speculative layer was visible or a section was enabled.

  1. Record one baseline.Choose a view, restore the intended layer combination, and note the geometry-status label before saving.
  2. Change one variable.For a sky experiment, hold the camera and sidereal time fixed while changing epoch. For a depth experiment, hold scale and viewpoint fixed while changing visible layers.
  3. Export the session or report.Retain the input identities and the settings named by the export. A source digest answers which available bytes were used; it is not a certification of the source's claims.
  4. Restore against the matching assets.A session is not a copy of the scene, catalogue or source library. Camera coordinates and DEF names need the compatible model frame and dataset.

A copied view link encodes the supported observation state and needs the same installed study. A session file adds a timestamp, frame description and available asset hashes. Import compares the scene hash when one is supplied; the remaining hashes are recorded for comparison. Notebook observations and copied links restore without the scene-hash comparison. If the clipboard is unavailable, export a session instead.

Camera capture restores position, orientation, centre and field of view when available; otherwise restoration uses the named viewpoint. Navigation mode, tour progress and discovered-card progress are not saved. Treat evidence labels and the model's limitations as part of the view, including during focus mode. The context label describes the selected view, so free camera movement may reveal additional tiers.

10 / Six experiments

Predict the result, then use the scene to test the prediction.

01 / First exploration

Choose the evidence layers

Visit the Osiris section, then the imagined Hall. Toggle the speculative layer and read the persistent status label. Describe which dimensions are published and which positions or forms are constructed.

Keep: a session plus one sentence explaining geometry status.

02 / Spatial reasoning

Move a north-side cut

Enable the section near the Osiris plane. Predict which features remain as Z decreases. Use the survey compass and signed coordinates to check the direction.

Keep: plane position and the retained half-space.

03 / Quantitative comparison

Name the depth before measuring

Compare the shaft-drop sum, the published section label and the 648 m proposal in the depth theatre. Explain why their endpoints are normalized graphically and why the first two are different quantities.

Keep: units, definition, source and comparison ratio.

04 / Astronomy

Separate rotation from time of day

Hold local sidereal time fixed and scrub epoch through a former 100-year boundary. Then hold epoch fixed and change sidereal time. Explain which motion each control produces.

Keep: astronomical epoch, historical label and LST.

05 / Visual interpretation

Compare appearance at one camera

Save a viewpoint and compare the three baked light states. Note their independent normalization. Lower resolution, then restore it while keeping exposure and camera fixed.

Keep: camera, light state, exposure and viewport.

06 / Builder

Rebuild and inspect a small change

Change one inspection label or overlay parameter in a separate working copy, rebuild the scene, and inspect the Git diff. Check DEF uniqueness, ROUTE endpoints, finite geometry and colour-buffer compatibility before judging the image.

Keep: source patch and validation output.

# Giza Evidence Atlas: enhanced edition

This edition extends the supplied **Giza v2: the Osiris Shaft, the Hall of Records and the sky** X3D study. It retains the original local scene, supplied source cards, catalogue data, raw light bakes and bundled X_ITE 16.6.0 renderer, while adding investigation controls, explicit geometry status and reproducible observation settings. The scene remains schematic; the enhancement produces no new archaeological field measurements.

`BASELINE-NOTICE-v2.md` preserves the uploaded notice **byte for byte**. It records the original edition's assertions, checks, source-handling history and credits, including wording corrected here. Its SHA-256 is `abd5716ce8bc829aad5cd904becb2fab0e9c5be049742bb5a2a43438116425c2`. Read this notice for the enhanced edition and the preserved notice for the historical record.

## What this edition changes

- Separate documented, theorized and speculative layers remain selectable. Speculation starts hidden; named speculative views can enable their relevant layer. A persistent context panel distinguishes the selected subject's evidence tier from the geometry's modelling status. Free camera movement can reveal several tiers at once.
- A local survey overlay, compass, datum and reference rings accompany a north-side clipping section and a depth-comparison theatre. Added viewpoints include survey, depth comparison and imagined Hall detail. The additional Hall ornament is explicitly invented.
- Sky controls use astronomical-year labels, an independent local sidereal-time setting, a named-star probe and continuous precession playback. `sky-math.js` projects rounded sample matrices to right-handed orthonormal rotations and interpolates them with shortest-arc quaternion SLERP. The matrices' ERFA/Vondrak provenance is inherited from the supplied data; this pass does not independently recompute them with ERFA.
- Exposure, resolution presets and focus mode support visual inspection. A twelve-slot browser-local camera notebook, view links, session import/export and JSON reports preserve supported observation settings. Reports include available asset hashes and distinguish historical validation from current structural checks.
- `STUDY-AUDIT.md`, `SKY-MODEL.md`, `evidence-index.json`, regression checks and the field guide make the interpretation and software contracts inspectable. The source cards and their historical opened/read flags retain their own scope; visible edition notes qualify the sky count and Khafre radar claim without rewriting those source records.

## Quantities, geometry and provenance

The local right-handed frame uses metres: **+X east, +Y up, +Z south**, with a schematic Sphinx origin and a flat model datum at Y=0. Underground positions in plan, chamber orientation, many dimensions and the surface context are simplified or invented as described in the baseline notice and the current evidence index. This is not a surveyed control network.

The Osiris shaft drops reported by Hawass are **9.62 + 13.25 + 7.50 = 30.37 m**. The **35.45 m** label in the published section uses the figure's ground datum. These are different quantities with different reference conventions, rather than rival measurements from one origin. The depth theatre normalizes endpoints for comparison; it is not a new survey section. The deep Khafre “648 m” visualization remains a disputed proposal, and the Hall of Records geometry, ornaments and depth remain imagined.

Evidence tier, geometry status and source-handling grade are distinct. In the supplied cards, “Confirmed” denotes a source opened and read; it is not a verdict that each claim is true. The baseline records 75 cards and 128 distinct source URLs, with 125 marked opened. That is a historical research record, not a claim that this enhancement reopened all links. The narrower checks made for this edition are identified in `STUDY-AUDIT.md` and `evidence-index.json`.

## Sky and appearance

The bundled data contain **978 field-star records, 40 named-star records and 152 epoch samples**, covering astronomical years −13000 through 2100. The baseline notice's “41 named” count was incorrect. Astronomical year 0 is 1 BCE; −10499 is 10500 BCE. The field catalogue is incomplete. Proper motion, refraction and a civil-time apparent-place pipeline are outside the current model; `SKY-MODEL.md` describes its numerical and astronomical limits.

The camera-to-`SkyRig` translation ROUTE already existed in the uploaded scene and generator. It is preserved here, rather than presented as a new fix. It keeps a finite-radius star shell centred on the moving observer, avoiding artificial travel parallax.

Three supplied Blender Cycles bakes provide morning, midday and afternoon appearance. The source state id `sunrise` uses azimuth 90° and elevation **+7°**; it is an illustrative morning state, independent of sidereal time and not a dated solar-rise calculation. Each bake has its recorded display normalization. The night state is blue-toned dimmed albedo, not a fourth Cycles bake. Exposure and image sampling change presentation, not light transport or archaeological accuracy. Original bake receipts remain in `states.json`.

# Giza v2: the Osiris Shaft, the Hall of Records and the sky (X3D study)

An X3D 4.0 world (Immersive profile) drawn by X_ITE 16.6.0 (bundled in `vendor/x_ite/`, with its own LICENSE.md), with a page that opens translucent glass modals in three lenses (Technical, Wonder, Contested) and a claim ledger. v2 of the 13 Nov 2026 study; v1 (`../v1/`) is untouched and still works. Geometry is generated by `build/build_scene2.py` and is CC0. Claims keep their own authors.

**What is new since v1.** Real Blender Cycles light baked into vertex colour for three times of day. A real star sky (the star catalogue from the x3d-blender-io demo page plus 41 named stars) that you can scrub from 13,000 BC to 2100 AD, with Orion's Belt, Leo and the Orion Correlation overlay. A walkable imagined Hall of Records interior, labelled speculative. Three lens modals (32 Technical, 21 Wonder, 22 Contested cards) where every Contested card is told as claim, then evidence. Primary papers opened where free (Hawass 2007, Hassan 1944, Petrie 1883, the Big Void and North Face Corridor papers). Ground truth fixed against v1 (below).

**Three tiers, three colours.** Documented (cyan), theorized (amber), speculative (magenta). Layers are `Switch` nodes (`SW_DOC`, `SW_THE`, `SW_SPE`, plus `SW_ORION`, `SW_GAZE`, `SW_SKY`, `SW_CONST`). The ledger in the page has 25 rows (17 documented, 3 theorized, 5 speculative), each with its basis and a grade (Confirmed: source opened and read; Likely: consistent across sources or computed here from such inputs; Suspected: a summary or recollection).

**What is invented (Confirmed).** Every underground position in plan (the three Osiris shafts are offset 4 m and 6 m from each other to read clearly); the heights of Chamber I and the sizes of the six side chambers (drawn as equal boxes; the text gives each its own size); the compass orientation of every chamber; the Khufu interior beyond published lengths (positions and the Big Void's location are schematic); the Sphinx silhouette; the box that stands for the seismic anomaly (thickness 3 m); the whole interior of Cayce's Hall (box size, 16 columns, 26 niches, dais, orb, light) and its 26 m depth; the radius, spiral pitch and cube positions of the "648 m" claim; the one-system links and pulses; the causeway and valley temple shapes; the colour grading of the Blender bakes (exposure rules in states.json).

**Corrections to v1 (left as published).** The Great Pyramid entrance is 7.29 m east of the axis (v1 drew it west) and starts on the casing 13.3 m in from the edge; the King's Chamber's long axis is east-west (v1 had it north-south); the Queen's Chamber box was transposed; the Osiris Shaft now sits under Khafre's causeway, about halfway from the Sphinx to the funerary temple (Hawass 2007 p. 381), not 110 m west of the Sphinx; Chamber A has a published height; the claimed cubes under Khafre are 90 m a side in the coverage read (v1 drew 80 m); the "12 x 9 x 5 m" anomaly is about 9 by 12 m in plan and buried less than 5 m, so 5 m is a depth bound, not a height; the unsourced 41.48 m depth is flagged instead of drawn as fact; Hawass 2007 is CASAE 36 (not ASAE 36); the Orion Correlation is Bauval 1989 and 1994; the Great Pyramid, Khafre and Menkaure heights are labelled as today or original.

**Sky.** `stars_v2.json`: 978 stars within 50 light-years (HYG v4.1, from the x3d-blender-io demo page `docs/brag/stars.json`) rotated into the ICRS frame with one rotation fitted on Sirius and Procyon (eleven other stars land within 0.16 degrees); 41 named bright stars from CDS Sesame; Vondrak 2011 long-term precession matrices (ERFA `ltpb`) every 100 years; proper motion ignored (Orion's Belt unaffected; Arcturus about 8 degrees off at 10,500 BC).
